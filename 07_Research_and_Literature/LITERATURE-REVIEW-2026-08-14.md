# FinReg-SLM — Literature Review (Phase 2 Investigation)

**Location note:** Moved into `Research/` on 2026-08-14 to keep all literature-search output in one place, separate from the project's own codebase/report artifacts at the repository root.

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (updated same day, second pass — §1.8 added)
**Scope:** Systematic search restricted to Scopus-indexed peer-reviewed journals and conference proceedings, grounding the five technical claims underlying FinReg-SLM's phase design. AI-assisted search (WebSearch/WebFetch); every source below was checked for a real, verifiable publication venue before inclusion. Sources that could not be confirmed beyond an arXiv preprint are listed separately in §3 and excluded from the evidentiary base per the "gray zone = fail" verification standard — they are not cited as peer-reviewed support.

**Research question:** What does the peer-reviewed literature establish about (a) retrieval vs. fine-tuning for domain knowledge injection, (b) retrieval-augmented fine-tuning (RAFT) for skill injection, (c) hallucination in legal/regulatory RAG, (d) catastrophic forgetting under parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and (e) temporal/version-aware retrieval for legal-regulatory text — and what exists specifically on Indian financial/regulatory NLP?

## 1. Confirmed Scopus-eligible sources (verified venue + citation)

### 1.1 Retrieval vs. fine-tuning for knowledge injection

**Ovadia, O., Brief, M., Mishaeli, M., & Elisha, O. (2024). Fine-Tuning or Retrieval? Comparing Knowledge Injection in LLMs.** *Proceedings of the 2024 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)*, 237–250. Miami, FL. ACL Anthology: `2024.emnlp-main.15`.
- **Verification:** Confirmed via ACL Anthology (official proceedings record) and the EMNLP 2024 accepted-papers list. EMNLP main-conference proceedings are Scopus-indexed as conference proceedings under Computer Science.
- **Finding:** RAG consistently outperforms unsupervised fine-tuning for knowledge injection, both for knowledge seen during pretraining and genuinely novel knowledge. Fine-tuned models exhibit the "Reversal Curse" (can answer a fact stated one direction, fail the inverse); RAG does not. This is the paper FinReg-SLM's Phase 1/2 briefs already cite as Ovadia et al. — the citation year in the project docs (2023 in one brief, 2024 in the other) should be standardized to **2024** (EMNLP main conference), with 2312.05934 as the arXiv preprint identifier if a preprint citation is also wanted.

**Soudani, H., Kanoulas, E., & Hasibi, F. (2024). Fine Tuning vs. Retrieval Augmented Generation for Less Popular Knowledge.** *Proceedings of the 2024 Annual International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval in the Asia Pacific Region (SIGIR-AP '24)*, Tokyo. ACM DL: `10.1145/3673791.3698415`.
- **Verification:** Confirmed via ACM Digital Library DOI record. ACM conference proceedings are Scopus-indexed.
- **Relevance:** A second, independently-run confirmation of the Ovadia et al. direction, specifically on long-tail/low-frequency entities — closer to the Indian-regulatory-text situation (largely absent from pretraining) than a general-knowledge benchmark would be. Worth adding alongside Ovadia et al. as corroborating evidence rather than relying on a single paper for the project's central premise.

**Balaguer, M. A. de L., Benara, V., de Freitas Cunha, R. L., et al. (2024). RAG vs Fine-tuning: Pipelines, Tradeoffs, and a Case Study on Agriculture.** arXiv:2401.08406.
- **Verification:** This is an **arXiv preprint** — I could not confirm a peer-reviewed venue for it. It is already cited in `Phase 1/00-PROJECT-BRIEF.md`. Per the Scopus-only standard, this should be treated as supporting/illustrative context, not as peer-reviewed evidentiary support, unless a conference/journal publication can be located. Recommend either dropping it from the paper's evidentiary citations or explicitly flagging it as a preprint in the write-up.

### 1.2 RAFT — retrieval-augmented fine-tuning

**Zhang, T., Patil, S. G., Jain, N., Shen, S., Zaharia, M., Stoica, I., & Gonzalez, J. E. (2024). RAFT: Adapting Language Model to Domain Specific RAG.** *Conference on Language Modeling (COLM 2024)*. OpenReview: `rzQGHXNReU`.
- **Verification:** Confirmed accepted/published at COLM 2024 (peer-reviewed conference, first held 2024). COLM is new enough that I could not independently confirm current Scopus indexing status — flagging this as **unconfirmed indexing, confirmed peer review**, distinct from the fully-confirmed EMNLP/ACM/Wiley sources above. If a strictly Scopus-indexed citation is required, EMNLP/ACM equivalents on the same technique should be sought as a supplement.
- **Finding:** RAFT trains the model to identify and cite the correct "golden" document verbatim while explicitly ignoring distractor documents in the retrieved context — the exact mechanism `Phase 3` claims to implement. Validated on PubMed, HotpotQA, and Gorilla API-call datasets, not on legal/regulatory text — Phase 3's application to Indian regulatory QA is a genuine domain extension, not a replication.

### 1.3 Hallucination in legal/regulatory RAG

**Magesh, V., Surani, F., Dahl, M., Suzgun, M., Manning, C. D., & Ho, D. E. (2025). Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools.** *Journal of Empirical Legal Studies*, 22, 216–242. DOI: `10.1111/jels.12413`.
- **Verification:** Confirmed via Wiley Online Library DOI and independently corroborated citation (SCIRP reference database, Stanford DHO preprint host). *Journal of Empirical Legal Studies* is a long-established Wiley journal, Scopus-indexed.
- **Finding:** First preregistered empirical evaluation of commercial RAG-based legal research tools (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research, Ask Practical Law AI). Hallucination rates of 17–33% despite retrieval grounding. This is the paper already cited in FinReg-SLM's Phase 2 brief as the evidence that "retrieval does not fix hallucination on its own" — confirmed accurate and correctly cited.

### 1.4 Catastrophic forgetting under (parameter-efficient) fine-tuning

**Luo, Y., Yang, Z., Meng, F., Li, Y., Zhou, J., & Zhang, Y. (2023). An Empirical Study of Catastrophic Forgetting in Large Language Models During Continual Fine-tuning.** *Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)*, 8934–8947.
- **Verification:** Confirmed EMNLP 2023 main-conference proceedings (page range independently corroborated). Scopus-indexed.
- **Finding:** Catastrophic forgetting is observed across the 1B–7B parameter range (directly covers FinReg-SLM's model scale), worsens with scale within that range, and general instruction-tuning beforehand partially mitigates it. This matches the citation already in `Phase 2/12-PHASE2-BRIEF.md` ("Luo et al. EMNLP 2023").

**Biderman, D., Portes, J., Ortiz, J. J. G., Paul, M., Greengard, P., Jennings, C., King, D., Havens, S., Chiley, V., Frankle, J., Blakeney, C., & Cunningham, J. P. (2024). LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less.** *Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)*.
- **Verification:** Confirmed publication in TMLR (OpenReview-hosted, peer-reviewed, "Featured Certification"). TMLR's Scopus-indexing status could not be independently confirmed via search — **flagging as unconfirmed indexing, confirmed peer review**, same caveat as COLM above.
- **Finding:** LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning on target-domain accuracy but forgets less on out-of-domain tasks than full fine-tuning, weight decay, or dropout — a genuine trade-off curve, not a free lunch. This directly supports (and slightly complicates) FinReg-SLM's use of QLoRA: the project should expect *some* target-domain ceiling loss from choosing LoRA over full fine-tuning, which is a reasonable trade given the 8GB VRAM constraint, but this should be stated explicitly as a design trade-off rather than implied to be cost-free.

## 1.6 Additional sources found via institutional library access (ScienceDirect + IEEE Xplore, NMIMS Mumbai)

The open-web search in §1.1–§1.5 undersells what's actually available — general web search surfaces mostly arXiv preprints because that's what's freely indexable, while the peer-reviewed venues that matter sit behind paywalls a college library subscription unlocks. Searching directly in ScienceDirect (Elsevier) and IEEE Xplore — both platforms Scopus indexes near-completely — surfaced stronger, more current, and more directly on-topic sources than the open-web pass did, especially for the legal/regulatory-RAG and Indian-legal-NLP threads.

**Ansar, W., Goswami, S., & Chakrabarti, A. (2027). LeSum: Cost-effective LLM-driven hybrid summarization of Indian legal judgments.** *Expert Systems with Applications*, 332(Part C), Article 133683. DOI: `10.1016/j.eswa.2026.133683`.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on ScienceDirect (full record, DOI, abstract). *Expert Systems with Applications* is a well-established, high-volume Elsevier journal, Scopus-indexed (Q1, CiteScore consistently in the top tier of its category).
- **Finding:** Hybrid extractive+abstractive summarization of Indian court judgments (20,273 Calcutta High Court judgments, self-curated dataset, plus the Indian Supreme Court judgments dataset), using PEGASUS/BART/Gemini/Llama via fine-tuning and zero-shot prompting, evaluated by three senior legal experts. This is the strongest available comparandum for "LLMs applied to a large corpus of Indian legal text at production scale" — genuinely worth citing as related work, and worth being able to explain the difference from FinReg-SLM (summarization of adjudicated case law vs. retrieval-grounded QA over regulatory circulars — related domain, different task).

**Liu, Q., Yu, H., Wang, Q., Xu, Q., Li, J., Zou, Z., Mao, R., & Cambria, E. (2026). Legal knowledge infusion for large language models: A survey.** *Information Fusion*, 125, Article 103426. DOI: `10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103426`. Open access.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on ScienceDirect. *Information Fusion* is a top-tier Elsevier journal (Scopus Q1, high impact factor), and this article is part of a themed special issue ("GenAI for Information Fusion").
- **Finding:** A structured survey distinguishing **full-parameter training infusion** vs. **low-parameter (PEFT) infusion** of legal knowledge into LLMs — i.e., exactly the fine-tuning-strategy axis FinReg-SLM's Phase 2→3 pivot sits on, but surveyed across the legal-AI literature broadly rather than one paper's result. This is a strong candidate to replace or supplement the project's reliance on Ovadia et al. alone as "the" fine-tuning-vs-knowledge literature — it's domain-matched (legal, not general-QA) and recent (2026).

**Hindi, M., Mohammed, L., Maaz, O., & Alwarafy, A. (2025). Enhancing the Precision and Interpretability of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in Legal Technology: A Survey.** *IEEE Access*, 13. Open access.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on IEEE Xplore (full record). *IEEE Access* is Scopus-indexed. Notably well-cited already: 63 citing papers, ~17,459 full-text views at time of access — a genuinely load-bearing survey in this sub-area, not an obscure one.
- **Finding:** Surveys RAG methods, stages, and techniques specifically in the legal domain, including retrieval-precision innovations, evaluation metrics, and emerging datasets/benchmarks — directly relevant to justifying FinReg-SLM's hybrid retrieval (dense+BM25+rerank+temporal filter) design choices with domain-specific rather than general-RAG literature.

**Chen, H., & Garner, P. N. (2024). Bayesian Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting.** *IEEE/ACM Transactions on Audio, Speech, and Language Processing*, 32, 4253–4262. DOI: `10.1109/TASLP.2024.3463395`. Open access, CC BY.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on IEEE Xplore. IEEE/ACM TASLP is a well-established, Scopus-indexed journal — a materially stronger citation than Biderman et al.'s TMLR venue for a strict Scopus-only requirement.
- **Finding:** Applies Bayesian Laplace-approximation regularization (EWC, KFAC, EKFAC) on top of LoRA fine-tuning and shows catastrophic forgetting can be substantially mitigated **without degrading fine-tuning performance** — Kronecker-factored (KFAC) regularization outperforms the simpler diagonal (EWC) approach. This is an actionable **mitigation**, not just a measurement: FinReg-SLM's own `Phase 2/src/finreg/train/forgetting.py` currently only fabricates a static "0.6–0.8 pt loss" number (see `PROJECT-STATUS-REVIEW-2026-08-14.md` §4.3) rather than either measuring or mitigating forgetting — this paper is the correct citation if a genuine mitigation strategy (e.g., EWC-regularized QLoRA) is ever implemented, and is worth citing regardless as the state of the art on the exact problem the project currently only asserts a number for.

**Zhou, Z., Jiang, T., & Jin, Q. (2026). Integrating fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation to address the application challenges of pollution control guidelines.** *Journal of Environmental Management*, 400, Article 128718. DOI: `10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.128718`.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on ScienceDirect (full record, DOI, abstract, highlights). *Journal of Environmental Management* is a well-established Elsevier journal, Scopus-indexed.
- **Finding — the single closest published analog to FinReg-SLM's Phase 3 found anywhere in this search.** Combines RAG with LoRA fine-tuning (base model Qwen3-8B) for interpreting **regulatory/technical guidelines** (Chinese Ministry of Ecology and Environment pollution-control documents) — structurally almost identical to what Phase 3 attempts, just for a different regulator. Reports a **63.8% average gain in lexical-overlap metrics and 10.5% gain in semantic performance** for hybrid RAG+FT over baselines, with expert review rating the hybrid approach best specifically on "long, structurally complex regulatory QA" — the same document shape (long, hierarchical, cross-referenced, tabular) as Indian regulatory circulars. **This creates a genuine tension worth addressing directly in the project's write-up**: this paper found FT+RAG clearly beats RAG alone on a comparable regulatory-guideline task, while FinReg-SLM's own Phase 2 found "no detected difference" (G1) — and per `PROJECT-STATUS-REVIEW-2026-08-14.md` §4.1, that Phase 2 result was produced by template-string stand-ins rather than real model generations. Citing this paper is a double-edged move: it strengthens the domain premise (FT+RAG *can* work for regulatory text) but also sharpens exactly the question an examiner would ask about why FinReg-SLM's own Phase 2 didn't detect the same effect — the honest answer is that Phase 2 never actually tested it (see the companion status review), which this paper makes harder to avoid addressing.

**Han, I.-S., Roh, M.-I., & Kong, M.-C. (2026). A RAG-based Q&A system for ship regulations applying domain adaptation.** *International Journal of Naval Architecture and Ocean Engineering*, 18, Article 100735. DOI: `10.1016/j.ijnaoe.2025.100735`. Open access.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on ScienceDirect. Journal is Scopus-indexed (Elsevier-published on behalf of the Society of Naval Architects of Korea).
- **Finding:** A RAG-based Q&A system for ship classification-society regulations, with a hierarchical database preserving text/equations/tables, domain-adapted BERT for retrieval, and instruction-tuned LLaMA for answer generation — architecturally close to FinReg-SLM's own dense+BM25+rerank+temporal-filter pipeline. A second strong structural comparandum (regulatory RAG system, different regulator) alongside the pollution-control paper above.

## 1.8 Foundational methodology papers and further small-LLM regulatory-RAG evidence (added 2026-08-14, second pass)

Found via direct institutional-database navigation (session persists on `svkm.mapmyaccess.com` without needing tabs re-shared) plus a targeted web-verification pass for the two canonical foundational-method papers neither ScienceDirect nor IEEE Xplore carries (both are NeurIPS proceedings, not Elsevier/IEEE).

**Dettmers, T., Pagnoni, A., Holtzman, A., & Zettlemoyer, L. (2023). QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs.** *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS)*, vol. 36, pp. 10088–10115.
- **Verification:** Confirmed via the official NeurIPS proceedings record (`proceedings.neurips.cc`) and independently corroborated citation (Scientific Research Publishing reference database, ACM DL cross-listing `10.5555/3666122.3666563`). NeurIPS proceedings are Scopus-indexed.
- **Why it belongs here:** This is not background reading — it is the exact fine-tuning method FinReg-SLM's Phase 2 and Phase 3 both use (`raft_train.yaml`: 4-bit NF4 quantization + LoRA adapters, i.e. QLoRA). The project's own methodology section should cite this directly rather than only citing papers that *apply* QLoRA to other domains; this is the paper defining the technique itself.

**Lewis, P., Perez, E., Piktus, A., Petroni, F., Karpukhin, V., Goyal, N., Küttler, H., Lewis, M., Yih, W., Rocktäschel, T., Riedel, S., & Kiela, D. (2020). Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks.** *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS)*, vol. 33, pp. 9459–9474.
- **Verification:** Confirmed via the official NeurIPS proceedings record and independently corroborated (dblp, UCL Discovery institutional repository).
- **Why it belongs here:** The original RAG paper — the foundational citation for the "R" in every RAG comparison already in this review (Ovadia et al., Soudani et al., and the whole retrieval-vs-fine-tuning literature build directly on this). It was a notable gap that this review discussed RAG extensively without ever citing the paper that introduced it.

**Pai, K.-C., & Hsu, W.-C. (2026). Fine-tuning small language models for industrial retrieval-augmented generation: Efficiency, factuality, and hallucination analysis.** *Computer Standards & Interfaces*, 98, Article 104163. DOI: `10.1016/j.csi.2026.104163`. Open access.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on ScienceDirect. *Computer Standards & Interfaces* is a Scopus-indexed Elsevier journal.
- **Finding — the best-aligned paper found in this entire search.** Fine-tunes six small LLMs (1.5B–8B parameters — directly spanning FinReg-SLM's actual 1.5B model) with QLoRA on real industrial RAG logs from two domains, one of which is **"internal regulation" documents** specifically. Reports that fine-tuned SLMs reach performance comparable to GPT-4o-mini, that 50–200 training samples are sufficient before returns diminish, and — most importantly for FinReg-SLM's own honest self-assessment — that **factual correctness on regulation-domain RAG (mean 0.488) is substantially harder and lower than on customer-service RAG (mean 0.661)**, with a proposed five-category hallucination taxonomy (fabrication, unsupported inference, misinterpretation, suspected hallucination, low faithfulness) built and validated against 154 human-rated samples. This is directly useful corroborating evidence for why FinReg-SLM's own real Arm A6 citation precision (51.6% among cited responses, per `PROJECT-STATUS-REVIEW-2026-08-14.md` §4.4) came out lower than the hoped-for 80–88% — an independent, peer-reviewed source confirms regulation-domain RAG is measurably harder than other RAG domains even for capable small models, not just for FinReg-SLM's specific implementation.

**New Mind AI Team (2025). Tailoring AI for Turkish Law: Domain-Specific Fine-Tuning of Small Language Models for Legal Expertise.** *Procedia Computer Science*, 267, pp. 124–135. DOI: `10.1016/j.procs.2025.08.239`. Open access (with a published corrigendum, `Procedia Computer Science`, 267, p. 271 — evidence of a real editorial/correction process, not a drive-by preprint).
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on ScienceDirect, including its corrigendum record.
- **Finding:** Fine-tunes an 8B SLM (Llama 3.1) across **ten distinct legal sub-domains — including Tax, Capital Markets, and Data Protection (KVKK, Turkey's GDPR-equivalent)** — using LoRA/QLoRA orchestrated via Axolotl and **Unsloth**, the exact same acceleration library FinReg-SLM's own `raft_train.yaml` uses (`unsloth/qwen2.5-1.5b-instruct-unsloth-bnb-4bit`). This is the closest structural precedent found for FinReg-SLM's own multi-regulator ambition (RBI/SEBI/CBIC/DPDP): a single base model, domain-adapted per regulatory sub-area, evaluated against the un-tuned base model with lexical, semantic, and AI-judge metrics. Its scope is Turkish law broadly, not financial regulation specifically, and it does not address retrieval or temporal versioning — a fine-tuning-only study, not RAG+FT — but it is strong evidence that the "one base model, per-domain adapters" pattern (which the project's own earlier slide-deck pitch called "Regulator adapters") is an active, real, peer-reviewed line of work elsewhere.

**Kim, Y. (2026). Detecting jurisdiction compliance gaps in process plant engineering with regulatory RAG.** *Journal of Loss Prevention in the Process Industries*, 103, Article 106100 (October 2026). DOI: `10.1016/j.jlp.2026.106100`.
- **Verification:** Author name, volume and full abstract confirmed directly on the institutional ScienceDirect record (session recovered after re-login; author is a single named author, Yuyong Kim — not a corporate byline as initially suspected). *Journal of Loss Prevention in the Process Industries* is a Scopus-indexed Elsevier journal.
- **Finding:** Integrates a Korean regulator's (KOSHA) public-API regulatory corpus — 1,327 guide documents and 3,102 statutory-provision rows, 16,174 searchable entries in a SQLite FTS5 index — into an engineering AI verification pipeline spanning seven engineering disciplines, to catch cases where a design passes international codes (ASME/API) but still violates jurisdiction-specific process-safety regulation (e.g., passing ASME B31.3 while violating Article 256 of Korea's Occupational Safety and Health Standards). Notably, **regulatory grounding is generated on-premises with a small model (qwen3:4b) served through Ollama** — the same small-model, on-premises, jurisdiction-specific-RAG pattern FinReg-SLM itself follows, just for a different country and industry. In a 60-scenario ablation, enabling the jurisdiction-specific regulatory layer raised detection of otherwise-missed compliance violations from 0/60 to 26/60; a curated retrieval benchmark showed enhanced retrieval improving Recall@1 from 0.44 to 0.74 over plain BM25. This is the clearest published precedent found for FinReg-SLM's own core premise — that international/general-purpose compliance checking is not equivalent to jurisdiction-specific regulatory compliance, and that a small, on-premises, dedicated regulatory-RAG layer measurably closes that gap. It is a different regulator and industry (Korean process-safety engineering, not Indian finance) and does not address temporal/version validity, but is otherwise the single closest architectural sibling to FinReg-SLM found across this entire search.

**Baqar, M., & Khanda, R. (2026). Hallucinations and Truth: A Comprehensive Accuracy Evaluation of RAG, LoRA and DoRA.** *2026 2nd International Conference on Federated Learning and Intelligent Computing Systems (FLICS)*. arXiv preprint: `2502.10497`.
- **Verification:** Conference venue and 2026 publication year independently confirmed via direct IEEE Xplore search earlier in this research pass (listed there as "2026 2nd International Conference on Federated Learning and Intelligent Computing Systems (FLICS), Year: 2026, Publisher: IEEE"). The arXiv preprint (posted Feb 2025, later presented at FLICS 2026) is cross-referenced for the abstract/methodology detail below. A specific article DOI could not be independently confirmed in this pass and is deliberately omitted rather than asserted.
- **Finding:** A large-scale empirical comparison of RAG, LoRA, and DoRA (Weight-Decomposed LoRA) across 20,000 FAQ-style queries against a 400,000-entry knowledge base. Finds each technique optimizes a different axis — RAG for knowledge grounding, LoRA for cost-efficient domain adaptation, DoRA for a efficiency/precision balance — directly relevant to justifying FinReg-SLM's own choice of combining RAG with QLoRA rather than treating them as competing alternatives, and a useful citation for explicitly considering (and could motivate testing) DoRA as an alternative to plain LoRA in future work.

**Hacker, P., & Holweg, M. (2026). The regulation of fine-tuning: Federated compliance for modified general-purpose AI models.** *Computer Law & Security Review*. DOI: `10.1016/j.clsr.2025.106234`. Open access.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly via the institutional ScienceDirect listing (author names, DOI and publication date — 30 April 2026 — captured before the session broke) and independently cross-referenced via SSRN, Semantic Scholar, and the Oxford University Research Archive. *Computer Law & Security Review* is a Scopus-indexed Elsevier journal. Volume/page numbers were not independently confirmed in this pass.
- **Finding:** Not a technical RAG/fine-tuning paper but a legal one — directly relevant because it analyzes how *fine-tuning itself* is treated under emerging AI regulation (the EU AI Act's general-purpose-AI-model provisions), proposing a taxonomy of technical modifications and a "federated compliance" framework spanning the AI value chain. This is worth a brief mention in FinReg-SLM's own discussion: the project fine-tunes a general-purpose open-weight model for a regulated financial-compliance use case, which is precisely the kind of "modified general-purpose AI model" this paper's compliance taxonomy addresses — a reflexive point (the tool built to help with regulatory compliance is itself a fine-tuned model that may one day face its own compliance obligations) worth acknowledging rather than ignoring, particularly in a Limitations or Future Work section.

## 1.10 Fourth pass — retrieval-pipeline methodology foundations (open web verification only, institutional session not retried)

FinReg-SLM's own hybrid retrieval pipeline (`Phase 2/src/finreg/retrieve/`) implements three specific, named techniques that had not yet been given their own foundational citations anywhere in this review: reciprocal rank fusion (`fusion.py`), cross-encoder reranking (`reranker.py`), and an LLM-as-judge evaluator (`Phase 2/src/finreg/eval/judge.py`). Two of the four canonical papers checked here are Scopus-confirmed; the other two are, perhaps surprisingly, still arXiv-only after several years — a genuine and worth-stating property of this literature, not a search failure.

**Cormack, G. V., Clarke, C. L. A., & Buettcher, S. (2009). Reciprocal Rank Fusion Outperforms Condorcet and Individual Rank Learning Methods.** *Proceedings of the 32nd International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval*, Boston, MA, 2009.
- **Verification:** Confirmed via dblp and the IR Anthology (independent bibliographic databases for the information-retrieval field). ACM SIGIR proceedings are Scopus-indexed.
- **Why it belongs here:** This is the exact algorithm FinReg-SLM's `retrieve/fusion.py` implements (`reciprocal_rank_fusion`) to combine dense and lexical (BM25) retrieval results. A methodology section citing the retrieval architecture should cite this directly rather than describing RRF as if it were a novel design choice — it is a 2009 result being correctly applied, not invented.

**Zheng, L., Chiang, W.-L., Sheng, Y., Zhuang, S., Wu, Z., Zhuang, Y., Lin, Z., Li, Z., Li, D., Xing, E. P., Zhang, H., Gonzalez, J. E., & Stoica, I. (2023). Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena.** *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS)*, vol. 36, Datasets and Benchmarks Track.
- **Verification:** Confirmed via the official NeurIPS proceedings record. NeurIPS proceedings are Scopus-indexed.
- **Why it belongs here:** The canonical, most-cited foundational paper for using an LLM as an automated evaluator — exactly the design FinReg-SLM's `LLMJudge` class implements, including the paper's own documented biases (position, verbosity, self-enhancement) that any implementation should account for and validate against, which is precisely why FinReg-SLM's own design includes a human-agreement validation step (κ ≥ 0.60 threshold) rather than trusting the judge blindly. This is a foundational-technique citation currently missing that should be added wherever the judge methodology is described.

**Xiao, S., Liu, Z., Zhang, P., Muennighoff, N., Lian, D., & Nie, J.-Y. (2023/2024). C-Pack: Packed Resources for General Chinese Embeddings.** arXiv:2309.07597.
- **Verification:** This is an **arXiv preprint** — despite being the paper that introduces the BGE embedding family (`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, the exact embedding model FinReg-SLM's `retrieve/dense.py` uses), no confirmed peer-reviewed publication venue was found for it in this search. **Excluded from the Scopus-confirmed evidentiary base** per the same standard applied throughout this review, despite BGE being one of the most widely deployed embedding model families in production RAG systems as of 2026.

**Nogueira, R., & Cho, K. (2019). Passage Re-ranking with BERT.** arXiv:1901.04085.
- **Verification:** Also **arXiv-only** — this is the foundational paper for BERT-based cross-encoder reranking, the technique FinReg-SLM's `retrieve/reranker.py` (`CrossEncoderReranker`) implements, and it remains, after several years and thousands of citations, formally unpublished at any peer-reviewed venue. **Excluded from the Scopus-confirmed evidentiary base** for the same reason.

**Rejithkumar, G., Pawar, S., Nair, P. P. M., Ramrakhiyani, N., & Anish, P. R. (2026). C-PASS: An organization-centric framework for Compliance and Penalty Assessment using Large Language Models.** *Information and Software Technology*, 196, Article 108148. DOI: `10.1016/j.infsof.2026.108148`.
- **Verification:** Confirmed directly on ScienceDirect via the institutional proxy, full text access, complete author list and abstract captured. *Information and Software Technology* is a well-established, Scopus-indexed Elsevier journal in software engineering.
- **Why this is one of the strongest finds in the whole search:** All five authors are affiliated with an Indian industry research organization (the paper's own text refers to "our organization," and the corresponding-author pattern and prior citation chain — e.g., "Rejithkumar et al. [26], [31]" self-citing earlier work on contract-element extraction — point to TCS Research, a well-known Indian NLP research group; verify the exact affiliation from the PDF's author block before citing it as such in a formal submission, since the HTML abstract view does not display affiliations). More importantly, the *methodology* overlaps with FinReg-SLM in ways no other paper in this review does simultaneously:
  - Extracts **temporal expressions** (deadline, lead time, duration, periodicity) from statutory clauses as a first-class structured output — not the same as FinReg-SLM's document-version temporal filtering, but the same underlying recognition that regulatory obligations carry meaning-critical temporal structure that generic text extraction misses.
  - Uses **GPT-4.1 as the core LLM component with an explicit "LLM-as-a-Judge" evaluation protocol** validated against expert human review — structurally the same evaluation design FinReg-SLM's own `LLMJudge` class implements (and the same design pattern grounded by the Zheng et al. 2023 paper in §1.10).
  - Frames regulatory compliance explicitly as a **software engineering concern** — "compliance obligations behave like non-functional requirements that must be engineered into systems" — which is a genuinely useful framing to borrow for positioning FinReg-SLM's own contribution.

This is worth being precise about: an Indian research group building compliance-extraction tooling for Singapore law is not the same claim as an Indian-jurisdiction system, and should not be conflated when citing this paper.
- **How to use it:** Cite it as the closest available evidence that (a) LLM-based regulatory-compliance tooling is an active, current (2026) area of Indian NLP research output, even if not yet applied to Indian regulatory text specifically, and (b) the LLM-as-judge validation pattern FinReg-SLM uses is an established, peer-reviewed-precedented methodology, not an ad hoc choice.

## 5. Novelty assessment — is FinReg-SLM a genuinely new contribution?

This was checked directly rather than inferred: I ran targeted searches against the institution's **full federated search** (`svkm.mapmyaccess.com`, covering ScienceDirect + Sage + Springer + ProQuest + Taylor & Francis + ASME + the library's own catalog simultaneously — seven databases at once, not one).

**Direct novelty test.** The query `"RBI SEBI compliance chatbot question answering artificial intelligence"` returned **zero results across all seven databases** — not "zero relevant," literally zero hits of any kind. Broadening to `"Indian banking regulation large language model retrieval augmented generation"` returned 111 ScienceDirect hits, none of which (based on titles/DOIs retrieved) is a system combining Indian financial-regulatory text with retrieval-augmented generation or fine-tuning — the results are financial-AI-adjacent (fintech adoption, IFRS/XBRL reporting, credit risk LLMs, generative AI in Indian agriculture) but not the specific combination.

**What this does and doesn't prove.** A zero-hit federated search across seven major databases is reasonably strong negative evidence — it means no one has published, in a venue any of these seven publishers cover, a paper whose title/abstract/keywords surface under these terms. It does **not** prove no one has built this as an internal industry tool (RBI/SEBI-adjacent compliance vendors plausibly have unpublished internal systems), and it doesn't rule out a paper existing in a database this library doesn't subscribe to (e.g., a pure-CS venue only in the ACM Digital Library without also being Scopus/ScienceDirect-indexed) — the earlier IEEE Xplore/ScienceDirect passes in §1.6 didn't surface one either, which corroborates rather than just assumes the negative result.

**Breaking the claim into its components — what's genuinely new vs. what's establishing precedent elsewhere:**

| Component of FinReg-SLM | Novel for India? | Precedent exists elsewhere? |
|---|---|---|
| RAG + LoRA fine-tuning combined for a **regulatory-guideline domain** | — | **Yes, directly** — Zhou et al. 2026 (pollution-control guidelines, China) and Han et al. 2026 (ship regulations) do exactly this pattern for other regulators. FinReg-SLM is not the first to try this *architecture*, but appears to be the first to try it for **Indian financial regulation** specifically. |
| Version-aware / temporal-validity-filtered retrieval for regulatory text | Largely yes | Thin — §3's preprint-stage papers (VersionRAG, Temporal Misgrounding in Legal RAG) show this is an active problem elsewhere, but no confirmed Scopus-indexed paper solves it for *any* jurisdiction yet, let alone India's. This is the strongest genuine novelty claim in the whole project. |
| LLM applied to a large corpus of **Indian legal/regulatory text** | Precedent exists for adjudicated case law (LeSum) | Not for regulatory circulars specifically — LeSum summarizes court judgments, a different document type and task than retrieval-grounded QA over RBI/SEBI circulars. |
| A version-aware, licence-tagged, provenance-complete **corpus** unifying RBI+SEBI+CBIC+India Code | Yes | No comparable published corpus was found; IndiaFinBench (preprint) is the closest attempt at an Indian financial-regulatory benchmark but doesn't claim to be a full acquisition-and-versioning corpus the way Phase 1 does. |
| The specific finding "fine-tuning adds ~0 over retrieval for Indian regulatory QA" | Would be novel | Only if actually measured — per `PROJECT-STATUS-REVIEW-2026-08-14.md` §4.1, this result was produced by template-string stand-ins, not real model comparisons, so it cannot currently be claimed as an empirical finding at all, novel or otherwise. |

**Bottom line:** the project is not "something already done" — no single existing paper combines Indian financial-regulatory scope, version-aware temporal retrieval, and RAFT-style skill fine-tuning. But it is also not novel *ex nihilo*: the individual techniques (RAG+FT for regulatory guidelines, RAFT for citation discipline, temporal filtering for legal text) each have direct precedent for other regulators/jurisdictions found in this search. The honest, defensible novelty claim is **combinatorial and geographic**: applying an increasingly well-established pattern (RAG+FT for regulatory-guideline QA) to a jurisdiction and corpus (Indian financial regulation, version-aware) that no confirmed peer-reviewed paper has covered — not "inventing a new technique." State it that way rather than implying the underlying method itself is unprecedented; an examiner who's read Zhou et al. or Han et al. (or finds them independently) will ask, and the honest framing survives that question while an overclaimed one doesn't.
